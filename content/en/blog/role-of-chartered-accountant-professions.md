> **In brief:**
> 1. Chartered accountant is a protected title reserved for members registered with the Ordre des experts-comptables.
> 2. No general legal requirement forces a small business to use a chartered accountant.
> 3. A regulated liberal profession follows practice and ethics rules set by an order or authority.
> 4. Self-employed nurses, for example, combine standard accounting obligations with rules specific to their professional order.

## What a chartered accountant actually does

A chartered accountant is a professional registered with the Ordre des experts-comptables, the only one entitled to use that title. They keep or supervise a business's accounts, prepare its annual financial statements, and advise on tax, social or management choices, under a precise professional and ethical liability.

### What is not a legal requirement

Using a chartered accountant is not a general legal requirement for a business. Some structures do without one entirely and keep their own books, either themselves or through dedicated software, at the cost of a higher risk of error.

## When a chartered accountant's help becomes useful

| Situation | Value of the support |
|---|---|
| Setting up a complex company | Securing structure and tax choices |
| Rapid revenue growth | Anticipating thresholds and filing obligations |
| Tax audit | Representing and defending the business's file |

## Regulated liberal professions

> "A regulated liberal profession is overseen by a professional order that governs its practice conditions, beyond standard accounting and tax obligations alone."
> — service-public.fr, 2026

Some liberal professions, such as self-employed nurses, combine standard accounting and tax obligations for the self-employed with additional rules set by their professional order: conditions for setting up practice, negotiated fee schedules, continuing education requirements.

## How to choose the right level of support

1. Assess the complexity of the situation: legal structure, invoicing volume, presence of employees
2. Compare the cost of a chartered accountant with the time self-managed bookkeeping would take
3. Check whether the activity falls under a regulated profession imposing specific accounting rules

This choice connects with decisions made as early as [business registration](/en/blog/company-registration-kbis/) and the [choice of legal structure](/en/blog/choosing-sas-sarl-legal-structure/), as well as tracking deadlines such as the [CFE local business tax](/en/blog/cfe-local-business-tax-france/).

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What does a chartered accountant do?</summary>

A chartered accountant, registered with the Ordre des experts-comptables, keeps or supervises a business's accounts, prepares its annual financial statements and advises on tax and social security choices, under professional liability.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is a chartered accountant mandatory for a small business?</summary>

No, there is no general legal requirement to use a chartered accountant, except in a few specific cases such as auditing certain associations' or structures' accounts. Many small businesses and micro-businesses do without one.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is a regulated liberal profession?</summary>

A regulated liberal profession, such as a self-employed nurse, is overseen by a professional order or authority that sets rules of practice, ethics and sometimes pricing, in addition to standard accounting and tax obligations.

</details>
