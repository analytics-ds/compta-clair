> **In brief:**
> 1. Gross pay is the basis used to calculate all employee and employer social contributions.
> 2. Net pay before tax equals gross pay minus employee contributions only.
> 3. Income tax is withheld at source directly on the payslip.
> 4. The 2026 French minimum wage (SMIC) sets the minimum reference for any salaried pay in France.

## The general structure of a French payslip

A French payslip follows a standardized order since the payslip was simplified: employer and employee identification, gross pay, contributions grouped by risk category, then net pay before and after tax.

### Mandatory information

Identity of both parties, the period and number of hours worked, job title, gross salary amount, breakdown of contributions, net amount paid, and payment date must all appear on the payslip.

## The breakdown of social contributions

| Contribution category | Finances | Employee share |
|---|---|---|
| Health | Health insurance | Small, mostly employer-funded |
| Retirement | Basic and supplementary schemes | Significant share |
| Unemployment | Unemployment insurance | Mostly employer-funded |

## From gross to net: the calculation steps

> "The simplified payslip groups contributions by risk category to make it easier for employees to read."
> — service-public.fr, 2026

1. Start from the gross salary agreed in the contract
2. Deduct employee social contributions to get net pay before tax
3. Deduct income tax withheld at source to get the net amount paid into the bank account

## The minimum wage as a reference

The French minimum wage (SMIC) sets the floor for any salaried pay in France and serves as a benchmark to check that a contract meets the legal minimum, including for part-time work calculated pro rata.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What is the difference between gross and net pay?</summary>

Gross pay is the salary before employee social contributions are deducted, while net pay is what remains after those deductions, before or after income tax withholding depending on which line is read.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why does a payslip show two net amounts?</summary>

The "net pay before tax" equals gross pay minus employee contributions, while the final "net paid" amount also deducts the income tax withheld at source, calculated according to the employee's tax rate.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is the minimum wage a gross or net amount?</summary>

The French minimum wage (SMIC) is always expressed as a gross hourly or monthly amount; the net amount received by the employee is lower once employee social contributions are deducted.

</details>
